1Word classes

Every English word belongs to a word class (also called a part of speech). The same word can belong to different classes: a walk (noun) and to walk (verb).

ClassJobExamples
Nounnames a person, place, thing or ideateacher, Stockholm, table, happiness
Pronounreplaces a nounhe, them, mine, who, itself
Verbaction, event or staterun, become, think, be
Adjectivedescribes a nouna big house, an interesting book
Adverbdescribes a verb, adjective or another adverbspeak slowly, very good, quite well
Prepositionshows place, time or relationin, on, at, with, for, between
Conjunctionjoins words, phrases or clausesand, but, because, although
Determinergoes before a noun and shows which or how manya, the, this, my, some, many
Interjectionshows a feelingOh! Wow! Ouch!

Parts of a sentence

Key terms
  • Subject (S): who or what does the action. Verb (V): the action or state. Object (O): who or what receives the action. Complement (C): information about the subject after be, become, seem.
  • Phrase: a group of words without a subject + verb (in the morning). Clause: a group with subject + verb. Sentence: one or more clauses.
  • Main clause can stand alone. Subordinate clause cannot (because it was raining).

The students (S) wrote (V) an essay (O).   The essay (S) was (V) long (C).

Common mistake: incomplete sentences

Because I was tired. (This is only a subordinate clause.)
I went home because I was tired.

2Sentences and word order

2.1 Basic order: S + V + O

English word order is fixed. The verb comes second in a normal sentence, but it does not move behind the subject when something else begins the sentence (unlike Swedish).

Swedish pattern (do not copy)English
Yesterday went I to school.Yesterday I went to school.
Then came she home.Then she came home.
Always I eat breakfast.I always eat breakfast.

2.2 Order of adverbials: manner – place – time

She spoke clearly at the meeting yesterday. (manner, place, time)

2.3 Frequency adverbs

always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never go before the main verb but after be and after the first auxiliary.

He often works late. He is often late. He has never been to Paris.

2.4 Negative sentences

Use do / does / did + not + base form. With be and modals, add not directly.

VerbNegative
I like tea.I do not (don't) like tea.   She does not (doesn't) like tea.
He went home.He did not (didn't) go home. (base form after didn't!)
She is happy. / They can swim.She is not happy. / They cannot (can't) swim.
I have finished.I have not (haven't) finished.
Common mistakes

He don't like it. → He doesn't like it.
She didn't went. → She didn't go.
I don't know nothing. → I don't know anything. (one negative only)

2.5 Questions

TypePatternExample
Yes/no (be, modal, have as auxiliary)Aux + S + V?Is she a teacher? Can you swim? Have they left?

2.6 Imperatives

Use the base form: Open the door. Don't be late. Let's go. For polite requests add please or use Could you …?

2.7 There is / There are, and it

English needs a subject in every sentence. When there is no real subject, use there or it.

There is a book on the table. There are two books. (verb agrees with what follows)
It is raining. It is important to sleep. It is 5 o'clock.
Is important to sleep.   Rains today.   In my town have a park. → There is a park in my town.

3To be, to have, to do

These three verbs are the most important in English. They work as main verbs and as auxiliary verbs (helping verbs).

3.1 Be

English always needs a verb

In some languages (for example Arabic) the verb be is not used in the present. In English it must always be written.

She very smart. → She is very smart.
The weather nice today. → The weather is nice today.

Be is also an auxiliary: I am working (continuous), It was built in 1990 (passive). Use be + adjective for age, feelings and weather: I am 17. I am cold. It is cold. I have 17 years. I have cold.

3.2 Have

  • Main verb: I have a car. British English also uses have got: I've got a car. Questions with main-verb have: Do you have a car? / Have you got a car?
  • Auxiliary in perfect tenses: She has finished. They had left.
  • Actions: have breakfast, have a shower, have a good time, have a look. Here you must use do / does in questions and negatives: Do you have lunch at 12?
  • have something done: I had my hair cut. (someone else did it)

3.3 Do

  • Auxiliary for questions and negatives: Do you smoke? She doesn't know.
  • Emphasis: I do like it!
  • Main verb: do homework, do the dishes, do your best.

3.4 Do or make?

do (activity, work, general)make (create, produce, choose)
do homework, do the housework, do the shopping, do a job, do business, do an exam, do someone a favour, do research, do your bestmake a mistake, make a decision, make an effort, make a plan, make money, make progress, make a noise, make a phone call, make a cake, make friends, make an appointment
Common mistakes

I did a mistake. → I made a mistake.   make homework → do homework   She have two brothers. → She has two brothers.

5Present simple and present continuous

5.1 Present simple

Form: base form. With he / she / it add -s: I work, she works. Questions and negatives with do / does: Does she work? She doesn't work.

Rule for he/she/itExamples
most verbs: add -swork → works, play → plays
ends in -s, -x, -z, -ch, -sh, -o: add -eswatch → watches, go → goes, fix → fixes, do → does
consonant + y: change to -iesstudy → studies, carry → carries (but play → plays)
irregularhave → has

Use: habits and routines (I get up at 7.), permanent facts (Water boils at 100°C.), timetables (The train leaves at 9.), and feelings/states (I love music.).

5.2 Present continuous

Form: am / is / are + base form + -ing. Use: actions happening now (I am reading.), temporary situations (She is staying with friends this week.), changing situations (The climate is getting warmer.) and fixed plans (We are meeting at 6.). With always it can show irritation: He is always losing his keys.

Adding -ing: spelling

make → making (drop silent e) · run → running, sit → sitting (double the last consonant after one short vowel) · lie → lying · play → playing · travel → travelling (UK).

5.3 State verbs (stative verbs)

Some verbs describe a state, not an action. They are normally not used in the continuous: know, understand, believe, remember, forget, like, love, hate, want, need, prefer, belong, own, seem, mean, contain, cost, depend.

I am knowing the answer. → I know the answer.   She is wanting a coffee. → She wants a coffee.

Some verbs have two meanings: I think it's good (opinion) / I am thinking about my holiday (action). She has a car / She is having lunch. I see what you mean / I am seeing the doctor tomorrow.

Common mistakes

He go to school every day. → He goes to school every day. (third person -s)
She is work in a bank. → She works in a bank. / She is working in a bank.
I am go to school now. → I am going to school now. (be + -ing)
Do he like pizza? → Does he like pizza? (does + base form; no -s on the verb)

6Past simple and past continuous

6.1 Past simple

Form: regular verbs + -ed (walked, studied, stopped); irregular verbs have special forms (go → went, see chapter 10). Questions and negatives: did + base form.

Did you see the film?   Did you saw the film?   I didn't go. I didn't went.

Use: finished actions at a known time (I met her in 2020 / yesterday / two years ago), a series of events in a story (He opened the door, walked in and sat down.) and past habits (We played football every day.).

Spelling of -ed

stop → stopped (double consonant) · study → studied (y → ied) · like → liked (add d) · play → played (vowel + y: no change). Pronunciation: /t/ after voiceless sounds (worked), /d/ after voiced (played), /ɪd/ after t and d (wanted, needed).

6.2 Past continuous

Form: was / were + -ing. Use: an action in progress at a past moment (At 8 last night I was cooking.), the background to a story (It was raining and people were hurrying.) and an action that was interrupted (I was cooking when she called.).

CombinationExample
when + past simple (short action interrupts)I was walking home when it started to rain.
while + past continuous (two long actions)While I was studying, my brother was playing music.

6.3 Used to and would

used to + base form: past habits and states that are no longer true: I used to live in Malmö. She used to be shy. Negative/question: I didn't use to smoke. Did you use to play? would + base form is only for repeated actions, not states: Every summer we would swim in the lake. I would live in Malmö. (state: use used to)

Common mistakes

I was born in 2008. I live here since then. → see chapter 7.
Yesterday I have seen her. → Yesterday I saw her. (finished time = past simple)
She was go to the shop. → She went / was going to the shop.

7Present perfect

7.1 Present perfect simple

Form: have / has + past participle (V3). Regular V3 = V2 (worked). Irregular: see chapter 10.

Use: a past action or state that is connected to the present. The time is not finished or not important.

UseExample
a state or action that began in the past and continues nowI have lived here for ten years. She has worked here since 2019.
experience in your life (ever / never)Have you ever been to London? I have never eaten sushi.
recent action with a result now (just / already / yet)I have just finished. She has already left. Have you done it yet?
unfinished time period (today, this week, this year)I have seen three films this week.
For, since, ago

for + length of time: for two hours, for a week, for years · since + starting point: since 2020, since Monday, since I was a child · ago + past simple: I started two years ago.

7.2 Present perfect vs past simple

Present perfectPast simple
no finished time mentioned, or time not finishedfinished time mentioned or understood
I have visited Paris. (in my life)
I have had three coffees today.		I visited Paris in 2019.
I had three coffees yesterday.
Rule of thumb

You can never use the present perfect with a word for a finished time: yesterday, last week, in 2010, two years ago, when I was a child, at 5 o'clock.

7.3 Present perfect continuous

Form: have / has been + -ing. It stresses the activity and its duration: I have been waiting for an hour. or a recent activity with a visible result: Your eyes are red. Have you been crying? Compare: I have written 3 emails (result, number) / I have been writing emails all morning (activity). State verbs (know, like) use the simple form: I have known her for years.

7.4 Been or gone?

She has been to Spain. (she visited and came back)   She has gone to Spain. (she is there now)

Common mistakes

I have seen him yesterday. → I saw him yesterday.
I live here since 2020. / I am here since Monday. → I have lived here since 2020. / I have been here since Monday.
I know her since 5 years. → I have known her for 5 years. (for, not since, with a length of time)
When have you arrived? → When did you arrive? (question with when = past simple)

8Past perfect

Form and use

Form: had + past participle (V3). Same for all persons: I had finished, she had finished. Negative: hadn't finished. Question: Had you finished?

Use: the earlier of two past events. The later event is in past simple.

When I arrived, the film had already started. (first: film starts, second: I arrive)

She was tired because she had worked all night.

I had never seen snow before I moved to Sweden.

Past simple + past simple (order of events)Past perfect + past simple (earlier first)
I got home. I ate dinner. (first home, then dinner)When I got home, my mum had already eaten. (she ate before I came)
When he called, I left. (he called, then I left)When he called, I had already left. (I left first)

Typical signal words

before, after, by the time, when, already, just, never … before, as soon as, until. After after you can use past perfect or past simple: After I had eaten / After I ate, I went out.

Past perfect continuous

had been + V-ing: a longer action that went on up to a past moment. She was red in the face. She had been running. We had been waiting for an hour when the bus came.

Reported speech and conditionals

Past perfect is also the "back-shift" tense in reported speech (ch. 14) and is used in the third conditional (ch. 13): If I had studied, I would have passed.

Common mistakes

When I came, he already left. → When I came, he had already left.

If I would have known, I would have helped. → If I had known, I would have helped.

I had went there. → I had gone there. (always V3 after had)

He said he has finished. → He said he had finished.

9Talking about the future

English has no single future tense. You choose a form depending on why you speak about the future.

FormUseExample
will + V1decision made now; prediction (opinion); promise, offer, threat; facts about the future"The phone is ringing." "I'll get it." · I think it will rain. · I'll help you. · I will be 18 next year.
be going to + V1plan made before speaking; prediction from evidence you can seeI'm going to study medicine. · Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.
present continuousfixed arrangement with time and place (often with a person involved)I'm meeting Sara at 6 tomorrow. · We're flying to Rome on Friday.
present simpletimetables, schedules, calendarsThe train leaves at 7.45. · School starts on Monday.
will be + V-ingin progress at a future moment; polite questions about plansThis time tomorrow I'll be flying. · Will you be using the car tonight?
will have + V3finished before a future momentBy June I will have finished school.
be about to + V1very soon, in a momentHurry up! The film is about to start.

Will or going to?

Use will for

• spontaneous decisions: I'll have the pasta.
• opinions / guesses: I think he'll win. (I believe, probably, perhaps)
• promises: I won't tell anyone.

Use going to for

• plans: I'm going to learn Spanish.
• predictions with evidence: He's going to fall!
• things you have already decided: We're going to move.

Time clauses: the big rule

Rule

After when, while, before, after, as soon as, until, by the time, if, unless, once we use a present tense for the future, never will.

When I arrive, I will call you.   If it rains, we will stay home.   I'll wait until you finish.

I will have left before you arrive.   As soon as she has finished, we'll go. (present perfect also works)

Common mistakes

When I will arrive, I will call you. → When I arrive, I will call you.

If it will rain tomorrow, … → If it rains tomorrow, …

I will to go. → I will go. (no to after will)

Tomorrow I go to the cinema. (if it is only a plan) → Tomorrow I'm going to the cinema.

I am knowing the answer tomorrow. → I will know the answer tomorrow.

11Modal verbs

Modal verbs: can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to. They are followed by the base form (no to, no -s, no -ing) and make questions and negatives without do.

She can swim. She cans swim.   Can he swim? Does he can swim?   I must go. I must to go.

12The passive

Form

be (in the right tense) + past participle (V3). The object of the active sentence becomes the subject.

Active: Shakespeare wrote Hamlet. → Passive: Hamlet was written by Shakespeare.

TensePassive formExample
present simpleam/is/are + V3English is spoken all over the world.
past simplewas/were + V3The window was broken yesterday.
present perfecthas/have been + V3The homework has been done.
past perfecthad been + V3The room had been cleaned.
present continuousam/is/are being + V3The road is being repaired.
past continuouswas/were being + V3I was being watched.
willwill be + V3The results will be announced on Friday.
modalmodal + be + V3This must be finished today. It can't be changed.
infinitiveto be + V3She wants to be invited.
-ing formbeing + V3I hate being told what to do.

When do we use the passive?

  • The doer is unknown or unimportant: My bike was stolen.
  • The action or result is more important than the doer: The bridge was built in 1998.
  • Formal / scientific writing: The samples were heated to 80 °C.
  • Use by + agent only when the doer is important or new information: Hamlet was written by Shakespeare.

Two objects

Verbs with two objects (give, send, show, tell, offer) have two passives: She gave me a book. → I was given a book. / A book was given to me.

Passive with reporting verbs

It is said that he is rich. = He is said to be rich. Also: believed, thought, known, expected, reported, claimed.

Have something done

When someone else does a service for you: have + object + V3. I had my hair cut. She is having her car repaired. (not I cut my hair, unless you did it yourself.)

Common mistakes

The accident was happened yesterday. → The accident happened yesterday. (happen, occur, arrive, die, fall, seem, exist have no passive)

The book writes by him. → The book was written by him.

It was cost 10 euros. → It cost 10 euros.

The letter was sended. → The letter was sent. (V3 of irregular verbs)

I am agree. → I agree.

13Conditionals and wishes

A conditional sentence has an if-clause (condition) and a main clause (result). If the if-clause comes first, put a comma. If it rains, we stay home. / We stay home if it rains.

TypeIf-clauseMain clauseMeaning · example
Zeropresent simplepresent simplealways true, facts: If you heat ice, it melts.
Firstpresent simplewill + V1real, possible future: If I study, I will pass.

28Writing a good text

28.1 Structure of an essay

Introduction

• Hook (question, fact, short story)
• Background
• Thesis: your main idea in one sentence

Body (2–3 paragraphs)

• One idea per paragraph
• Topic sentence
• Explanation, example, link back to the thesis

Conclusion

• Restate the thesis in new words
• Sum up your main points
• Final thought (no new arguments)

28.2 PEEL for a paragraph

PPointThe main idea of the paragraph
EEvidencefact, quote or example
EExplainwhy this supports your point
LLinkconnect back to the question / thesis

28.3 Style

  • Vary sentence length. Mix short sentences with longer ones that use linkers.
  • Be specific. A lot of people → about 60 % of teenagers. Prefer exact verbs: say → claim, explain, argue.
  • Avoid "thing", "stuff", "good", "bad", "very". Use beneficial, harmful, extremely useful.
  • Formal writing: no contractions (do not), no slang, no "you" for general statements (people / one), no exclamation marks.
  • Informal writing (blog, email to friend): contractions and short phrases are fine.
  • Be concise. Delete words that add nothing: in my personal opinion → in my opinion.
  • Active voice is usually stronger than passive.

Read your text 6 times, each time looking for one kind of mistake. This works much better than reading once and looking for everything.
